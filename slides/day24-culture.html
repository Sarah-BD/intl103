Inward FDI & culture

The other direction

  • Most of this course followed Western firms operating abroad. Now we flip it: foreign firms investing in the United States (inward FDI)
  • Inward FDI brings jobs and capital — but also clashes over norms, labor relations, and expectations
  • The host workforce, not just the host government, shapes whether a venture succeeds

Meyer’s map

Being the boss in Brussels, Boston & Beijing

Erin Meyer’s point: leadership expectations are culturally specific, and a style that works in one place can fail in another.

Dimension One end Other end
Leading Egalitarian (flat) Hierarchical (status matters)
Deciding Consensual Top-down

The trap: managers read others’ behavior through their own cultural defaults — and misattribute friction to personality or competence.

American Factory

Fuyao Glass comes to Ohio

  • The Chinese glassmaker Fuyao reopens a shuttered GM plant near Dayton, employing American workers under Chinese ownership and management
  • Frictions surface over the pace and intensity of work, safety, and unionization — and over very different expectations on each side
  • Automation looms in the background, raising the question of how durable these jobs are

From the business side: the Fuyao case

  • Today’s Hsu et al. excerpt gives you Fuyao’s side of the same story the documentary tells — how the firm itself assessed U.S. country risk before and during the Ohio investment
  • Read it as a companion, not a duplicate: the documentary shows the shop floor; the case shows the boardroom calculation behind investing in a market whose labor culture and regulatory environment were unfamiliar to Fuyao’s Chinese management
  • This is inward FDI’s liability of foreignness from Day 3 — playing out for a Chinese firm entering the U.S., the mirror image of the Western-firms-abroad cases you’ve read all term

Synthesis

What it shows

  • Inward FDI delivers jobs, but surfaces deep cultural and labor-relations frictions
  • Culture is not a soft add-on — it shapes productivity, safety, conflict, and the bottom line
  • The same power/authority/autonomy questions apply inside the firm, not just between firm and state
